## How to read the claim status

This release keeps four categories distinct:

- **Established prior art** — existing traditions, research or institutions acknowledged and linked as intellectual neighbours; these are not claimed as project inventions.
- **Project synthesis** — Simon Delaile's current arrangement, translation or integration of established and developing ideas.
- **Hypothesis** — a proposition that may be useful but has not yet been empirically established by this project.
- **Unvalidated proposal** — a candidate practice, institution, distinction or pilot awaiting external critique and real-world evaluation.

Human–AI development work, internal critique and editorial review do not constitute independent validation.

## The question that started the work

A recurring public claim about advanced AI is that it may radically increase productivity, reduce the amount of human labour required, and potentially contribute to forms of material abundance.

Taking that possibility seriously creates a second question:

> **If we believe technological capability may substantially change work and material production, what human and social foundations should we build before that transition — especially when many of those foundations would improve lives even if the predicted future arrives slowly, unevenly, or not at all?**

Human Foundations began from dissatisfaction with criticism alone. Rather than only asking why technology leaders were not preparing more visibly for the human consequences of the futures they described, the project asked what such preparation might actually require.

This document is a candidate answer, not a final one.

## Central proposition

> **Technological abundance may make far greater human freedom possible. But technical freedom is not automatically usable human freedom.**

A society can create formal options that people cannot meaningfully access, understand, afford, navigate or experience as their own.

Human Foundations therefore distinguishes three provisional dimensions:

- **Structural agency** — options, rights or freedoms formally exist.
- **Usable agency** — a person has sufficient access, capability, information, security and practical opportunity to exercise them.
- **Experienced agency** — a person experiences themselves as a meaningful author and participant in their own life.

These are project terms, not validated psychometric scales. They should be challenged against existing capability, human-development, autonomy and agency traditions.

## Why the future of work is larger than employment numbers

Paid work frequently carries functions beyond income: routine, social contact, identity, recognition, competence signals, belonging and a culturally legible form of contribution.

Those functions are not universal, and employment is not their only possible source. Work can also be exploitative, alienating, inaccessible or harmful.

The transition problem is therefore not simply:

**How do we replace lost wages or retrain displaced workers?**

It is also:

**If the social role of paid employment changes, what other institutions allow people to belong, learn, contribute, develop capability and remain recognised without making economic productivity the price of human worth?**

## Candidate Human Foundations

The exact set should remain open to evidence and criticism. Current candidate functions include:

- material security and equitable practical access;
- accessible lifelong learning and capacity discovery;
- critical thinking, epistemic literacy and the ability to challenge information and authority;
- relational and conflict literacy;
- plural forms of identity, belonging and contribution beyond employment;
- social infrastructure that permits connection without compulsory sociability;
- accessible public spaces for curiosity, making, teaching, repair, exchange and community;
- human–AI systems that expand capability while preserving human judgement, correction and authorship;
- user-owned and correctable cognitive continuity where digital systems support memory or long-term interaction;
- participatory design informed by people living under different material, cultural, disability and social conditions.

This list is intentionally provisional. A framework that absorbs every determinant of flourishing would become too broad to test or use.

## A low-regret criterion

Human Foundations should not depend on a speculative post-work future being correct.

A proposed foundation is especially interesting when:

1. it would help people under present conditions;
2. it could increase resilience or agency during technological transition; and
3. it does not require coercing people into one prescribed model of a good life.

That makes the project partly a search for **low-regret preparation**.

## One candidate implementation: Curiosity Commons

Curiosity Commons is a separate pilot project, not proof that Human Foundations is correct.

**Separate-project and rights boundary:** Curiosity Commons appears here only as an unvalidated candidate implementation. Its name, principle and any separate specifications, implementation materials or future project work are not included in this release's CC BY 4.0 grant.

Its core principle is:

> **Belonging comes first. Contribution may grow from belonging, but it is never the price of belonging.**

A Curiosity Commons is imagined as a shared environment where people can learn, make, teach, ask questions, repair things, exchange knowledge, eat, socialise or simply be present without needing a job, project or productivity target in order to belong.

It overlaps substantially with libraries, learning commons, makerspaces, community centres, cooperatives, mutual aid, volunteering and other established forms of social infrastructure. The claim is not invention of those traditions.

The current pilot question is narrower:

> **Can belonging be kept prior to contribution while capacities remain discoverable and connection remains voluntary?**

A first pilot should test the human/social mechanism before adding participant-facing AI.

## What this project is not claiming

Human Foundations is **not**:

- a new general theory of freedom or flourishing;
- a prediction that paid employment will disappear;
- an argument that employment is inherently harmful or inherently necessary for meaning;
- a claim that existing capability, human-development, just-transition, labour, social-infrastructure or lifelong-learning traditions ignored people;
- a validated policy framework;
- evidence that Curiosity Commons works;
- a justification for compulsory participation, forced volunteering or surveillance of belonging;
- permission to override people because a system infers stress, vulnerability or reduced capacity;
- a claim that AI uniformly expands agency;
- a claim that its individual ingredients are historically novel.

## Existing intellectual neighbours

This project should borrow from rather than reinvent substantial existing work, including:

- the capability approach and human-development tradition;
- just-transition and labour-market institutions;
- social protection and decent-work traditions;
- research on the non-income functions of employment;
- social infrastructure, community development and loneliness policy;
- adult and lifelong learning;
- accessibility and disability inclusion;
- human-centred AI, augmentation, agency and contestability;
- commons governance, cooperatives, mutual aid and participatory institutions.

Recent work makes the overlap particularly clear. UNDP's *Human Development Report 2025* centres AI on people's choices, capabilities and agency, including investment in capabilities and complementarity between humans and AI. In 2026, the ILO has similarly argued that the future of work will be shaped by policies, institutions and social dialogue rather than technology alone. Brookings has proposed a coordinated AI-work policy framework spanning guardrails, steering, safety-net buffers and structural shifts including rethinking paid work.

Those developments weaken any claim that the broad human-centred premise is novel. They sharpen the narrower question of whether the Human Foundations configuration adds useful integration, evaluation, overlooked dependencies or low-regret experiments.

## Current contribution hypothesis

> **Human Foundations may be useful as an integration and evaluation frame for asking whether high-capability AI transition is being designed with enough coordination across usable agency, the non-income functions currently carried by work, social-connection infrastructure, accessible lifelong learning, plural forms of contribution and agency-preserving human–AI systems.**

A useful outcome need not be a new framework. External criticism may show that the project is better understood as:

- a dependency map across existing literatures;
- a transition-evaluation checklist;
- a cross-field translation vocabulary;
- a generator of low-regret pilot questions; or
- a way to expose where technically available freedom remains practically inaccessible.

It is also possible that existing frameworks already perform these functions adequately. That is a legitimate result.

## Serious counterarguments

The project currently accepts at least these risks:

1. **Over-broad synthesis.** Integration can become a way of including everything and therefore explaining nothing.
2. **Paternalism.** Designing support for agency can itself reduce agency if institutions decide what people ought to need.
3. **Compulsory belonging.** Concern about loneliness or social fragmentation does not justify forced participation.
4. **Work romanticisation.** Employment's non-income functions are heterogeneous and should not be treated as universally beneficial.
5. **Technology determinism.** AI futures will be uneven, politically mediated and uncertain.
6. **Existing-institution duplication.** Curiosity Commons may reproduce functions already supplied effectively by libraries, community centres, makerspaces and other institutions.
7. **Agency-expansion assumptions.** AI can increase capability in one context while increasing dependency, exclusion, surveillance or vendor control in another.
8. **Integration without evidence.** A coherent diagram is not evidence that coordinating the components produces better outcomes.

## A challenge to technological-abundance claims

The originating challenge remains worth asking publicly:

> **Those who argue that advanced AI may create unprecedented productivity or abundance inherit a corresponding question: what investment in human capability and social infrastructure should occur before that transition, rather than only after disruption becomes visible?**

This is not addressed only to technology companies. Governments, researchers, labour institutions, educators, community organisations, funders and citizens all have standing in the question.

Nor does accepting the question require accepting the prediction. If many proposed foundations are useful now, their value can be tested independently of the most ambitious AI forecasts.

## What should happen next

This working framework is being released for criticism rather than presented as settled.

The next useful loop is:

**external critique → correction → bounded pilots/evaluation → evidence → revision**

Questions especially welcomed:

1. Which parts are already adequately captured by existing frameworks?
2. Does the structural / usable / experienced agency distinction clarify anything, or create unnecessary terminology?
3. Which proposed foundations are actually load-bearing, and which should be removed?
4. What important populations, conditions or institutions are missing?
5. Where does the framework risk paternalism or an implicit model of the “good life”?
6. Is the integration useful enough to change evaluation or policy decisions?
7. What is the smallest empirical test that could show one of its central assumptions is wrong?
8. What existing work should this project read before making stronger claims?

## Governing boundary

The purpose is not to decide for people what a technologically changed life should mean.

It is to ask whether people will have enough real capability, access, security, social infrastructure and institutional power to participate meaningfully in deciding that for themselves.
