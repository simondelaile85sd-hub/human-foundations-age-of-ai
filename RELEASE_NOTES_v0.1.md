# Public Working Framework v0.1 — release notes and audit record

**Creator:** Simon Delaile  
**Release date:** 14 September 2026  
**Status:** Developing, pre-validation, open to critique and prior-art correction

## Purpose of this release

This is the first public working release of *Human Foundations for an Age of AI*. It establishes a dated, inspectable record for criticism, prior-art correction and collaborative development. It is not presented as a validated policy framework.

## Final publication audit

The release was checked immediately before publication for:

- **Sources:** the three contemporary institutional claims were checked against the named UNDP, ILO and Brookings records; direct source links were added. The capability-approach neighbour is linked to a scholarly reference overview.
- **Privacy:** no private conversations, internal archives, addresses, contact details, lived-case material or third-party personal information are included.
- **Provenance:** Simon Delaile is identified as creator; the human-originating and AI-assisted development process is disclosed without treating AI assistance as independent validation.
- **Rights:** CC BY 4.0 is limited to Simon Delaile's original Human Foundations material in this release. Third-party material, the private website, unreleased work, future versions and the separate Curiosity Commons project are excluded.
- **Claim status:** established prior art, project synthesis, hypotheses and unvalidated proposals remain distinct. Novelty claims were not strengthened.
- **Validation:** the framework remains explicitly pre-validation. Internal critique, research mapping and editorial checking are not represented as empirical or independent validation.

## Deliberately not public

This release does not include the private website, private project files, conversations, internal archives, third-party personal information, lived cases, other project families, prototypes, books, or unreleased Curiosity Commons materials.

## Future revision rule

Corrections, counterevidence and prior art should change later versions visibly rather than being silently absorbed. Historical versions should remain available so the development path can be inspected.
